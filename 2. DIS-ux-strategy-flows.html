01 / Foundation

UX Strategy — Design Principles

Six core principles govern every design decision in DIS. They are not aesthetic guidelines — they are constraints that ensure a data-heavy, trust-sensitive system remains human-centered at every touchpoint.

Principle 01
Clarity Over Completeness

Complex driving data, ML scores, and blockchain records are never shown raw. Every data point is translated into a plain-language outcome. The user sees "Your braking was 23% smoother this week" — not the G-force values behind it.

Application: Driving score breakdown uses horizontal bars with simple labels. Premium calculations shown as "You pay ₹X less because you drive well." Technical details available on demand via progressive disclosure, never upfront.

Applied to: Score page, Premium breakdown, Claim settlement
Principle 02
Radical Transparency

Every automated decision — score change, premium adjustment, claim calculation — must be fully explainable. Users should never feel that the system "decided" something without them understanding why.

Application: Score changes accompanied by a one-sentence reason ("Harsh braking detected on 3 trips"). Premium adjustments show the exact formula in plain language. Blockchain record accessible but abstracted as a "Verified" trust badge.

Applied to: All score updates, all premium changes
Principle 03
Earned Trust

Trust is not declared — it is built through consistent, honest system behavior. The platform earns trust by being transparent about data use, never surprising users with unexplained changes, and providing verifiable records.

Application: "Why did my score drop?" always has an answer. Data consent is granular, not all-or-nothing. Blockchain verification shown as proof of immutability, not as a marketing claim.

Applied to: Onboarding, Score, Claims, Privacy settings
Principle 04
Behavior-Driven Feedback

The system's primary function is not just insurance — it is a behavior change platform. Feedback loops must be immediate, specific, and actionable. Vague feedback ("Drive better") is never acceptable.

Application: Alerts link directly to the specific trip and behavior. Score improvements show a delta ("↑ 4.3 points this week") with the specific action that caused it. Reward events are tied to explicit milestones, not random.

Applied to: Dashboard, My Score, Notifications, Rewards
Principle 05
Minimal Cognitive Load

The system holds enormous amounts of data. The interface never transfers that burden to the user. Decisions are pre-digested — the platform tells users what matters, when it matters, without requiring them to explore to find it.

Application: Dashboard surfaces one key number (today's score) not ten. Insights are singular — one actionable tip per session. Premium comparison shows three options maximum. Claims form has step-by-step structure, not one overwhelming form.

Applied to: Dashboard, Analytics, Claims, Renewal
Principle 06
Positive Framing

A system that charges people more for bad behavior must be careful about tone. Negative framing destroys engagement. Every penalty is reframed as an opportunity. Every low score is accompanied by a path to improvement.

Application: "Your score is below average" becomes "Improving these 2 habits could save you ₹800/year." Claim deductions shown with the score threshold for getting the bonus instead. No red warning banners on the home screen.

Applied to: All score feedback, claim outcomes, empty states

Four Key UX Challenges — With Design Solutions

🔢
Making Complex Data Understandable
OBD data includes G-force values, RPM readings, and multi-axis acceleration — incomprehensible to a non-technical driver. ML confidence scores and blockchain hashes are meaningless to end users.
UX Solution
All raw data is translated to three layers: (1) plain outcome — "Your braking improved," (2) a normalized 0–100 score, (3) a financial impact — "This saved you ₹X." Raw data accessible only via an "Advanced View" toggle. ML confidence shown as "Data quality: Good/Fair/Low" not a decimal number.
🤖
Building Trust in Automated Decisions
When an algorithm reduces a claim payout by 20%, users feel powerless and cheated. Without visible reasoning, automated systems feel arbitrary and hostile — destroying retention.
UX Solution
Every automated decision has a "Why did this happen?" link that opens a plain-language explanation. For claims: the exact blockchain record showing the driving score on the day of the incident is surfaced. Users can see — not just be told — the reason. Dispute pathway always visible.
🧠
Avoiding Information Overwhelm
The platform generates vast amounts of driving data across multiple sessions per day. Showing all of it creates paralysis and disengagement — users stop checking.
UX Solution
Dashboard surfaces exactly three things: today's score, one behavior insight, one financial impact. All other data lives one level deeper. Notifications are curated — only triggered when data crosses meaningful thresholds, not for every event. Weekly digest replaces daily data flood.
🔄
Encouraging Behavior Change Without Frustration
Drivers who receive only negative feedback ("Your score dropped again") disengage quickly. Punitive framing causes defensive responses, not behavior change. Conversely, vague positive feedback doesn't drive improvement either.
UX Solution
Feedback is always specific and improvement-oriented: "3 harsh braking events detected — easing off 15 meters earlier than usual would eliminate these." Streak mechanics reward consistency over perfection. Score improvement is celebrated more loudly than absolute score level.
01c / Experience Strategy

Experience Strategy

How the product communicates, what feedback loop it creates, and what emotional state it leaves users in — these are not incidental. They are designed intentionally.

🎯
Core Feedback Loop
Drive safely → OBD captures data → ML generates score → Score updates dashboard → Score reduces premium → User feels rewarded → User drives more safely. This loop must be completed within 24 hours of any trip for the feedback to be behaviorally meaningful.

How Insights Are Communicated

  • Outcome-first, data-second: "You drove 18% more smoothly" before showing graphs
  • Comparative framing: "Better than your last 3 trips" beats an absolute score number
  • Financial translation: Every score insight has a ₹ equivalent shown clearly
  • Single most important insight shown first — never a list of 8 findings
  • Negative insights always paired with a specific, actionable next step

How Feedback Loops Work

  • Immediate (in-trip): Future feature — real-time gentle audio cues
  • Same-day: Score updates within 2 hours of trip end — user checks app
  • Weekly: "Your week in review" digest — biggest win + biggest opportunity
  • Monthly: Premium impact preview — "At this rate, you'll save ₹X this year"
  • Renewal: Full year summary tied to exact premium discount earned

Emotional Design Goals

  • Confidence: User understands their score and how to improve it
  • Control: User believes their behavior directly controls their premium
  • Fairness: User trusts that the system treats them based on their actual driving
  • Progress: User feels they are improving over time, not just being judged
  • Pride: Streak badges and top-tier status create identity, not just savings

Design Priority
Users arrive at this flow under emotional stress. Every design choice must reduce anxiety: one step at a time, clear progress, no jargon, explicit explanation of how the driving score on the incident day affects their settlement — before they submit, not after.

02E / Edge Cases

Error & Edge Case Flows

Every failure point must have a recovery path, a plain-language explanation, and a next step. Dead ends are not acceptable. The goal is to convert every error state from frustration into a guided recovery.

📡
OBD Device Not Connected
Trigger
Dashboard loads but OBD device is not paired or Bluetooth is off. No driving data available for the current session.
System Response
Dashboard shows last available score with timestamp: "Score last updated 2 days ago — connect your device to refresh." Non-blocking banner at top: "Car not connected — tap to reconnect." Score ring shown in muted state, not hidden.
Recovery: Tap banner → step-by-step reconnection guide → device found → score sync begins. Alternative: "Upload trip data manually" always visible as fallback.
🚫
No Driving Data Available
Trigger
New user hasn't driven yet, or user hasn't driven in 14+ days. ML model has no data to score.
System Response
Empty state with illustration: "Your score appears after your first drive." Not a blank white screen — shows what the score will look like when populated, with clear motivation: "Drive today to see your first score and estimated premium."
Recovery: Contextual education shown while waiting — "Here's how your score is calculated." Premium preview shown using average score: "Most drivers save ₹X — drive safely to claim yours."
⚠️
Incorrect or Anomalous Driving Data
Trigger
OBD reports data that falls outside normal parameters (e.g., corrupted telemetry, speed reading of 800 km/h, or incomplete trip log due to Bluetooth drop).
System Response
Anomalous trip flagged automatically. Score not updated from corrupted trip. Notification: "One trip could not be scored — data quality issue detected. This trip has been excluded from your score." Never silently drops data.
Recovery: User can view the excluded trip, see the specific issue, and flag for manual review. Support ticket auto-generated. Score calculated from remaining valid trips.
❌
Claim Rejection Scenario
Trigger
Claim is rejected — due to invalid documentation, no active policy, or fraud detection flag triggered by anomalous data pattern.
System Response
Rejection never shown as a bare status. Full context: specific reason, the exact data or rule that triggered the rejection, and what the user can do. Dispute option always available. Adjuster contact available for manual review.
Recovery: "Resubmit with updated documents" (one tap), or "Dispute this decision" opens a review request form. Timeline for dispute review shown. User never feels abandoned.
🌐
Offline / No Network State
Trigger
User loses internet connectivity while using the app — during score loading, payment processing, or claim submission.
System Response
Cached data shown immediately. Offline banner: "You're offline — showing your last known data." All read operations work offline. Write operations (payments, claim submission) held and retried on reconnect.
Recovery: On reconnect, pending operations auto-retry with a notification: "Your claim was submitted successfully." Any form data entered while offline is never lost — stored locally first.
🔒
Blockchain Sync Delayed
Trigger
Driving score has been calculated but the blockchain record hasn't been committed yet (Hyperledger Fabric network delay or peer endorsement pending).
System Response
Score shown with "Pending verification" badge instead of "Verified." Not hidden — shown with context: "Your score is calculated and accurate — blockchain verification in progress (usually <2 min)." User is not alarmed.
Recovery: Badge auto-updates to "Verified" when sync completes. No user action required. Insurance and claims remain functional during the brief window — score itself is trusted even before blockchain confirmation.
03 / Interaction Design

Interaction Design Layer

Micro-interactions are not decoration — they are the system's communication layer. Every state change must be acknowledged, every success celebrated proportionally, and every failure explained immediately.

⭕
Score Update Animation
Score ring sweeps from previous value to new value (0.8s, easing cubic-bezier). Number counts up to new score. Color transitions smoothly through tier boundaries. Delta badge pops in: "↑ 4.3" with green accent. If score drops: amber delta, no alarming red — neutral improvement framing maintained.
🔔
Alert Notification Behavior
In-app banners appear at top of screen for 6 seconds then slide out. For score alerts: banner has score delta and one-line explanation. Tap opens detail, swipe dismisses. Push notifications for: score improvement milestones, claim status changes, renewal reminders. Never more than 2 notifications per day from any category.
✓
Form Confirmation Feedback
CTA buttons show loading state immediately on tap (spinner replaces label text). Never double-tappable. Success: checkmark animation (0.4s) before page transition. Payment success: full-screen green checkmark (1s) — proportional to the high-stakes nature of the action. Never a bare "Success" toast for payments.
⏳
Loading & Empty States
Skeleton screens instead of spinners — structural placeholders prevent layout shift. Empty states always have an illustration, a headline explaining the empty condition, and one CTA. Score calculation in progress: pulsing ring + "Calculating your score…" with contextual education below. Never a blank white void anywhere in the app.
📝
Error Message Patterns
Three error levels: (1) Inline validation — red text below the specific field, no modal, (2) Non-blocking banner — amber, dismissible, shows for 8 seconds, (3) Blocking dialog — only for irreversible actions (delete, permanent data changes). Error messages always: name the problem + explain what to do. No system codes. No "Something went wrong."
🎉
Reward & Milestone Events
Score milestone (80%+, 90%+): confetti burst animation, celebratory headline: "Excellent driver status unlocked!" Token awarded with pop animation. Streak milestones (7, 30 days): badge animation in dashboard. Premium discount applied: distinct green banner: "Your score just saved you ₹X this month." These moments are given full-screen treatment proportional to their significance.
Immediate (<2s)
Real-Time Feedback
Form validation, button states, OBD connection status, payment processing confirmation. All UI state changes acknowledged within 100ms of user action.
Same-Day (2–4h)
Trip Processing Feedback
Score update after trip completion, behavior insight card generated, blockchain record committed, notification sent. All within 2–4 hours of trip end.
Weekly Digest
Progress Review
Week summary: biggest improvement, biggest opportunity, premium projection. Delivered Sunday evening. Single notification, single screen. Never more than 3 data points per digest.

Positive Reinforcement Mechanisms

  • Variable ratio rewards: Token rewards don't follow a fixed schedule — this creates higher engagement (same mechanism as effective habit apps)
  • Streak protection: After a bad driving day, streak is shown as "at risk" with a recovery path for the next day — not immediately broken
  • Progress anchoring: Score shown relative to personal best, not just absolute value — "3 points from your best ever score" motivates more than a number alone
  • Micro-wins: Even a 0.5-point improvement shown as a win: "Your smoothest Monday drive in 3 weeks"
  • Social proof (optional): "Drivers like you save an average of ₹1,200/year" — no competitive leaderboard unless opted in

Avoiding Negative Frustration Patterns

  • No punishment framing: Score drops shown as "opportunity to recover" not failures
  • Attribution clarity: Score drop always explained: "3 hard braking events on Tuesday's commute" — never mysterious
  • No shame mechanics: Never compare unfavorably to other users without consent. Never show national rank prominently
  • Grace periods: First 7 days labeled "Learning period" — score shown but not counted for premium calculation yet
  • Context sensitivity: Alerts about driving in bad weather or heavy traffic are suppressed or contextually softened — system understands external factors

05 / Trust Architecture

Trust & Transparency Design

In a system where algorithms directly affect financial outcomes, trust is not a feature — it is the foundation. Every layer of the product must actively build it. Users who trust the system improve their driving and retain their policies. Users who don't, churn.

🔐
Trust Architecture Principle
Trust is built through: explanation (why did this happen?), evidence (here is the data that supports it), and control (here is what you can do about it). All three must be present for every automated decision the system makes.
📈
Communicating Score Changes — "Why Did My Score Change?"
Every score delta must be accompanied by a specific, factual explanation. Users never face a score drop without an answer. The explanation follows a three-part formula: What happened (3 harsh braking events) → Where it happened (Tuesday 8:23 AM, near [area]) → How to fix it (ease off 80m earlier from the signal). This is not a notification feature — it is the product's core trust mechanism.
"Your score dropped 3.2 points — 3 harsh braking events were detected on your Tuesday morning trip. Braking more gradually near MG Road junction will prevent this."
🛡️
How Premiums Are Calculated — Full Transparency
Premium calculation is never a black box. The Insurance page shows the complete calculation in plain language with all components labeled. Every number is explainable: the base premium, how the driving score tier maps to a multiplier, the tax components, and the stamp duty. An interactive "What if" slider shows the user exactly what their premium would be at different score levels — giving them direct, visualizable control over their cost.
"At your current score of 84%: Base ₹6,000 × 0.75 discount = ₹4,500 + CGST ₹405 + SGST ₹405 + Stamp Duty ₹1 = ₹5,311 total."
🔒
Data Privacy Communication — Granular, Honest, Respected
Data consent is collected granularly (not all-or-nothing) and explained in plain language at each permission request. The privacy page uses a simple table: what data, who sees it, how long it's kept. Data sharing with third parties (insurers, MVD) is shown explicitly with toggle controls. Annual "Your Data Review" notification reminds users what data the system holds and allows bulk deletion requests. Privacy is designed in, not bolted on.
"Your driving score is shared with Bajaj Allianz (your insurer) and the Motor Vehicle Department. It is not sold to any third party. You can revoke sharing anytime in Settings → Privacy."
⛓️
Blockchain Trust — Without Technical Overload
The blockchain is the system's most powerful trust tool — but only if users understand what it means without needing to understand how it works. The UI communicates blockchain verification through a simple, recognizable trust badge: a checkmark shield labeled "Verified Record." Tapping it reveals a plain-language explanation: "Your driving score is stored on a secure network that cannot be changed by anyone — not by you, the insurer, or DriveSure." Block timestamps shown in human-readable format, not as hexadecimal hashes. The "Blockchain" word itself is used sparingly — "Verified Record" is more user-meaningful.
"Verified on 21 May 2023 at 10:54 AM · Score: 84.07% · This record cannot be altered by any party. Your insurer and the MVD can view but not modify this record."
